The problem

Where you grow up shouldn't decide what you can learn.

The UK brass band tradition is a significant part of British cultural identity, built by working-class communities in industrial towns. It is under pressure, and the places that built it now have some of the thinnest opportunity in the country. Whether a young player gets a proper teacher, or any way into banding at all, depends almost entirely on geography and luck. The tradition needs a dependable way in.

What's on the platform

Built for the way brass players actually learn.

A pathway in, not a destination. School of Brass meets players where they are, from first long notes to contest-day nerves, and points them toward the bands and teachers who can carry them further.

1

Technique courses

Filmed lessons from top-seat players, covering every chair in the band.

2

Searchable library

Stuck on triple tonguing? Search it and watch a top player break it down. Plus performances, interviews and the stories behind banding.

3

Section progression

Everyone starts in 4th Section and climbs by completing courses, up through 3rd, 2nd and 1st to Championship. It measures your own learning journey, whether your band contests or not.

4

Legendary band pairing

Reach Championship and wear the crest of a legend: Black Dyke, Grimethorpe, Brighouse, Fairey, Cory, Foden's.

5

Phone first design

Ten minutes on the bus or a full Sunday session, it meets you there. Save your place, pick up anywhere.

6

Commissioned recordings

Six top players filmed up close, plus a full band recorded with the conductor talking you through the performance.

How it works

Subscriber funded. Creator shared. Built to last.

Between £5 and £10 a month at launch, rising gently as the catalogue grows. A share of every subscription goes straight to the musicians who teach on it. The build is proposed for funding through an Arts Council England National Lottery Project Grant. From launch, banding funds its own.
